# omniKPI deck style

Use this skill for competition decks, project pitches, proposal presentations, and important project reviews in this repository. Read [the reference analysis](../../../Deck_Example/STYLE_NOTES.md) for the observed examples. For the ITASE 7.0 final, also read [the deck requirements](../../../Deck_Context/ITASE_DECK_REQUIREMENTS.md), [the SDG 9 position](../../../Deck_Context/SDG_9_POSITION.md), and [the current system notes](../../../Deck_Context/CURRENT_SYSTEM.md).

## Direction

Make the deck feel like a considered competition presentation: modern, professional, direct, evidence-led and technically credible. The reference decks share strong blue/navy accents, bright content pages, bold sans-serif headings, a visible presentation structure, and a story that moves from a real problem to a solution and its proof.

Treat the references as visual and narrative guidance, not as templates to copy. Do not reuse their logos, product claims, imagery, slide text or distinctive layouts. Adapt the cues to omniKPI's brand, audience and time limit.

## Writing and story

- Give each slide one point. Prefer an assertion as the headline over a generic section name. The supporting material should prove or explain that assertion.
- Start with a recognizable person or workflow and a concrete friction point. Move from problem to proposed change, system flow, demo evidence, feasibility, impact and close.
- Use specific language and evidence. Put sources near claims, label synthetic data and assumptions, and separate what runs today from what is proposed.
- Use numbers only when their origin and scope are clear. Keep caveats near illustrative figures rather than burying them in an appendix.
- Write for spoken delivery: short phrases on slides, natural transitions in speaker notes, and enough detail in an appendix for technical Q&A.
- Keep slide copy in the language requested for that presentation. The references mix Indonesian and English by audience; do not mix languages just for decoration.

## Visual cues to carry forward

- Use a white or very light content canvas with a restrained navy and saturated blue system. Use pale blue for grouping and a contrasting accent only when it serves the data or brand.
- Use strong sans-serif display headings and a readable body face. Maintain a clear size hierarchy and generous contrast.
- Keep a compact section-progress cue or page marker when it helps the audience locate the current part. Avoid a large persistent navigation strip if it competes with content.
- Prefer one useful visual per slide: process diagram, evidence screenshot, comparison table, chart, architecture view, roadmap or a small set of labeled metrics.
- Use real product screenshots for product claims. Use diagrams for system behavior and tables for structured comparison. Keep labels close to the object they explain.
- A dark or photographic cover can create a strong opening. Keep subsequent content slides clear and readable; decorative imagery must not compete with the argument.
- End with a concise conclusion and a clear thank-you or discussion slide. Put detailed sources, technical design, assumptions and backup analysis in the appendix when useful.

## Restraint

The references sometimes use dense cards, repeated boxes and small text. Treat these as examples to edit down, not defaults. Do not make every slide a card grid. Vary the composition according to the evidence, and remove decorative framing that does not help the audience understand the point.

Before delivery, inspect every slide at presentation size. Check that the story is clear in sequence, body text is readable, charts and diagrams have labels, citations are legible, and nothing is clipped or overlapped. If the user supplied a time limit or rubric, map every required criterion to visible slide content and leave enough room to present it aloud.
